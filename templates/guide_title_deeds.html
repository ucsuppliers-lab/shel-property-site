In Cyprus the title deed is the proof of who owns a property. The Department of Lands and Surveys issues it, and every listing on Shel Property says where the deed stands. There are three answers you'll see.

Title deed issued

The property has its own deed, in the seller's name. This is the simplest case. Your lawyer still searches the Land Registry to check there's no mortgage, charge or court order on it, and that the building matches its planning permit.

Deed after completion

You'll see this on new builds. The developer can only apply for separate deeds once the building is finished and has its final approval, and that can take a long time. Until then, your lawyer lodges the sale contract at the Land Registry, which protects your claim to the property. They should also check whether the developer has a mortgage on the land, and how it will be released.

Title deed pending

This is a resale where separate deeds haven't been issued yet, often on a complex built some years ago. You can still buy, with the contract lodged at the Land Registry, but ask how far the application has got and what's holding it up. Your lawyer can find out.

Before you pay a deposit

  • Use your own lawyer, not one recommended by the seller or the developer.
  • Ask the lawyer for the Land Registry search in writing.
  • Make sure the sale contract is stamped and lodged at the Land Registry.
