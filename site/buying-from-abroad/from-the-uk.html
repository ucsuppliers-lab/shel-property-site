Buying in Cyprus

Buying in Paphos from the UK

Draft guide. A Cyprus lawyer checks every figure here before the site goes live. General information only, not legal or tax advice.

British buyers topped the Interior Ministry's figures for Paphos in the year to September 2025, with 890 purchases. Since Brexit, though, you buy as a non-EU citizen. That adds a permit, and changes the rules on how long you can stay.

The permit to buy

Non-EU buyers need permission from the Council of Ministers. Your lawyer applies to the District Officer for the area on form Comm.145. It usually takes one to two months.

How much you can buy is less clear. Lawyers' sources disagree on the limits, so we don't quote a figure. Ask your lawyer before you commit to more than one home or a large plot.

Parliament and the government are working on new limits for non-EU buyers, including bans on farmland and land near the Green Line, and caps on the number and size of homes. Nothing had passed when we last checked on 23 October 2026. See where the rules stand.

Staying in Cyprus

Owning a home doesn't give you the right to live in Cyprus. To stay longer than 90 days in any 180, you need a residence permit. One route is the investor's Immigration Permit: a new home bought from a developer for at least €300,000 plus VAT, and a secure income from abroad of at least €50,000 a year, plus €15,000 for a spouse and €10,000 for each child. There are other conditions, and the Migration Department decides.

VAT and the 5% rate

New builds carry VAT at 19%. If the home will be your main and permanent residence, part of it can be charged at 5%, up to set limits on size and price, and the Tax Department has to agree. Holiday homes don't qualify. How the 5% rate works.

What it costs

On a resale you pay Land Registry transfer fees; on a new build you pay VAT instead. Then legal fees. Our calculator works them out on any price.

Protecting yourself

  • Use your own lawyer, never one the developer or seller suggests.
  • Have your lawyer get a Land Registry search certificate dated just before you sign. It shows any mortgage or claim on the property.
  • Check whether the home has its own title deed. If not, find out why, and when it's due.
  • Have your lawyer lodge the contract at the Land Registry. That protects you if the seller tries to sell or mortgage the home again.
  • Send money through a currency transfer firm authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority, and confirm bank details by phone before every payment.

Tax back home

The UK and Cyprus have a double tax treaty, so you shouldn't pay tax twice on the same rent or gain. How it works for you depends on where you're resident. Speak to a UK tax adviser before you buy.

Where British buyers look

Peyia, Tala and Konia for views and an established British community; Polis, Argaka and Latchi for a quieter coast. Homes in Peyia · Tala · Polis
